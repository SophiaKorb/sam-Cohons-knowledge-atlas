# Temple-era research: institutional successor and independently indexed collection routes — October 31, 2026

**Scope:** Further research into the missing Rabbi Samuel M. Cohon Temple Emanu-El manuscripts (2000, 2001, 2015), Hearing Voices HV009 (2008), and original 2013 Temple class episodes. Research branch only. No copyright or private material ingested and **no** merge, release, deployment or contact.

## Substantial new route: Kol Ami is the documented successor to Tucson's Temple Emanu-El

The current [Kol Ami Synagogue institutional history](https://www.katucson.org/synagogue-history) identifies the 1910 Temple Emanu-El and Congregation Or Chadash as the legacy communities from which Kol Ami was formed, and says Rabbi Samuel M. Cohon joined Temple Emanu-El in **1998**. The historical account states that he served for approximately 19 years, followed by Rabbi Batsheva Appel. The legacy Temple community's Country Club Road premises were used by the successor synagogue.

A separately published [August 13, 2023 Pima County Interfaith interview](https://www.pimacountyinterfaith.org/kol_ami_synagogue), describing a conversation with Kol Ami leaders, is more legally precise: it states the prior two synagogues were **dissolved over a three-year period and replaced** by Kol Ami, rather than simply merged into one continuing legal organization. This identifies an organizational **successor research avenue**, not confirmation that institutional sermon papers, audio masters, or prior digital systems transferred.

## Guard against misleading archival matches

Web searches for Temple Emanu-El, Cohon, and recorded sermons surfaced several genuine but **wrong** collections. These have now been disambiguated in the source ledger:

- [University of Arizona MS 505](https://lib.arizona.edu/special-collections/collections/temple-emanu-el-sisterhood-tucson-arizona-records): genuine Tucson *Temple Emanu-El Sisterhood* material **1910–1941**, too early to document a 2000–2015 original without additional evidence.
- [San Diego State MS-0561](https://archives.sdsu.edu/repositories/2/resources/85): **Rabbi Morton J. Cohn**, whose Temple Emanu-El refers to a **San Diego** congregation, not Rabbi Samuel M. Cohon's Tucson congregation.
- [American Jewish Archives finding aids](https://www.americanjewisharchives.org/research/finding-aids/) identify **Samuel S. Cohon**, a different historical rabbi (1888–1959); those papers cannot be presented as those of **Samuel M. Cohon**.

This is a substantive search quality protection: a perfectly legitimate archive can still belong to the wrong person, era or location.
